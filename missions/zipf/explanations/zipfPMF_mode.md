For $N\geq1$ and $s\geq0$, rank $1$ maximizes the mass:

$$
\operatorname{zipfPMF}(N,s,k)\leq\operatorname{zipfPMF}(N,s,1)
\quad\text{for every natural }k.
$$

The harmonic normalizer is positive because its rank-one summand is $1$ and all other summands are nonnegative. On the support, $k\geq1$ and $s\geq0$ imply $k^s\geq1$, hence its reciprocal is at most $1$. Dividing by the common positive normalizer preserves this inequality. Off the support the mass is zero, which is at most the positive mass at rank $1$.
